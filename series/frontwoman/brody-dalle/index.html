Brody Dalle: the whole story punk forgot to tell

Brody Dalle wrote the songs, played guitar and fronted The Distillers, one of the fiercest punk bands of the 2000s. In 2003 she released Coral Fang, one of the best-reviewed punk albums of the year, and the scene spent the next decade talking about her personal life instead. This six-part Frontwoman series is about the music, and about what it cost her to make it.

Part 1 8 May 2026

Punk rock has a misogyny problem

Brody Dalle is one of the most uncompromising women ever to pick up a guitar. She wrote her own songs, fronted her own band and did not soften a single thing. Her songs ended up in Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, Spider-Man and Gran Turismo, and critics called her the real deal.

Still, the conversation kept drifting back to the men around her. This series asks why she keeps being framed as a supporting character in her own story.

Part 2 9 May 2026

Melbourne, two chords and a festival stage

Dalle grew up in working-class Fitzroy in Melbourne and has spoken publicly about a very difficult childhood. An uncle taught her two chords on an old acoustic guitar. She found Nirvana at twelve and Discharge at thirteen, formed the all-girl punk band Sourpuss at fourteen, and at sixteen played a major Australian festival.

Backstage at that festival she met Tim Armstrong of Rancid, who was about thirty. She was a minor. Within two years they were married and she was living in Los Angeles. The scene that later judged her for leaving that marriage had nothing to say about how it began.

Part 3 10 May 2026

The music outgrew the network

The Distillers' debut came out on Hellcat Records, a label owned by Armstrong. The access was his, and that matters. But the songs were hers. A harsh Village Voice review of the debut's lyrics pushed her to become a sharper songwriter, and Sing Sing Death House followed, with City of Angels and Seneca Falls.

The band toured with No Doubt and Garbage. Then Sire Records, the label that launched the Ramones and Talking Heads, signed them. With producer Gil Norton they made Coral Fang, and by its release Dalle was the only original member left. She built the band, rebuilt it and carried it.

Part 4 14 May 2026

Most Hated Woman on Earth

Then a photograph changed the conversation: Dalle had left Armstrong and was seen with Josh Homme. She has described her marriage as very controlling and said it took her years to leave.

Coral Fang stopped being discussed as an album and became a divorce headline. A man in her position would have had a messy month. She got a messy decade. She named the tour that followed the Most Hated Woman on Earth Tour. That was not self-pity. It was naming what was happening while it happened.

Part 5 15 May 2026

Spinnerette, Diploid Love and the years in between

The Distillers did not end in one moment. They eroded under years of touring, public pressure and substance problems that Dalle has been honest about. After the birth of her daughter she went through a long period of postpartum depression and has said she did not know who she was.

She had every reason to stop. She came back with Spinnerette, then released the solo album Diploid Love in 2014, a full decade after Coral Fang. Rolling Stone gave it four stars. The talent was still there. The scene had stopped looking.

Part 6 17 May 2026

The album no one has heard

In 2018 the Coral Fang lineup of The Distillers reunited and released the single Man vs. Magnet on Jack White's Third Man Records, their first new music in about fifteen years. In 2019 they recorded a full album, which Dalle called a masterpiece.

Then the pandemic cancelled the tour, followed by years of personal and legal difficulty. The album has never been released. Her life is not ours to judge. The talent was never in question, and the music deserves to be heard louder than the noise.
