Shirley Manson: the rooms changed, she didn't

Shirley Manson, from Edinburgh, fronted Garbage through the 1990s alternative boom, sang a James Bond theme and is still touring at sixty. For much of her career the press wrote about how she looked instead of what she made. This five-part Frontwoman series follows the decade before Garbage, the golden years, the label vote that sidelined the band, and the return on their own terms.

Part 1 18 September 2026

Who she is and why she matters

Garbage sold millions of albums, recorded a James Bond theme and headlined festivals around the world, with a Scottish woman from Edinburgh at the front of an American alternative band. For most of that career, the conversation around her had little to do with any of it.

The 1990s music press wrote about her in degrading, sexualised terms, and as the decades passed, ageism joined the sexism. She kept talking anyway: direct, articulate, and not waiting to be invited to the table. A new generation of artists, including Hayley Williams, has named her as an influence.

Part 2 19 September 2026

The decade before Garbage

At seventeen she joined Martin Metcalfe's band Goodbye Mr Mackenzie and spent ten years on keyboards, touring Europe, opening for Debbie Harry and touring with the Ramones. She has said that without Goodbye Mr Mackenzie there would have been no Garbage.

When that band stalled, she was offered a solo deal and brought her bandmates with her. The new band, Angelfish, released an album in 1994. Its video for Suffocate Me aired late at night on MTV's 120 Minutes, where Steve Marker saw it and showed it to Butch Vig and Duke Erikson. Her first session with them went badly. She went home, kept working on the songs, called Erikson with an idea, and that is how she joined Garbage.

Part 3 25 September 2026

The golden years and the 90s music press

Garbage's debut arrived in August 1995 and put trip-hop beats, industrial noise, rock guitars and pop hooks in the same songs: Vow, Only Happy When It Rains, Queer, Stupid Girl. Version 2.0 followed in 1998 with Push It, I Think I'm Paranoid and Special, and went to number one in the UK.

The more visible she became, the more the press sold the suggestion of sex before the songs. Courtney Love was too chaotic, Alanis Morissette was the angry ex-girlfriend, Shirley Manson was the fantasy. The music came second. Critics also dismissed Garbage's technology as artificial. If they were going to look at her, she was going to make them listen.

Part 4 27 September 2026

Beautiful Garbage and the breaking point

In 1999 Garbage sang The World Is Not Enough, the theme of that year's James Bond film. Their next album, Beautiful Garbage, explored gender, identity and desire in songs like Androgyny and Cherry Lips. The Androgyny video came out on 10 September 2001, the album three weeks later, and its promotion stalled in the weeks after the attacks.

Garbage were then folded into Interscope's larger system, and Manson has said the label chose to invest in No Doubt rather than Garbage, as if there were only one slot for a female-fronted rock band. Bleed Like Me reached number four on the Billboard 200 in 2005, but by the end of that tour the band were exhausted and went on hiatus.

Part 5 29 September 2026

The silence years, the return and Chinese Fire Horse

During the hiatus Manson recorded solo material that her label rejected, and in 2008 she played Catherine Weaver, a shape-shifting liquid-metal Terminator, in Terminator: The Sarah Connor Chronicles. Garbage returned in 2012 on their own label, Stunvolume, with Not Your Kind of People, followed by Strange Little Birds in 2016 and No Gods No Masters in 2021.

On the first day of promoting No Gods No Masters, a journalist asked her when she was going to retire. Her older bandmates had never been asked. On the 2025 album Let All That We Imagine Be the Light, she answered with Chinese Fire Horse, a song about ageism. Karen O has called her a mentor. She turned sixty in 2026, and Garbage are still touring.
