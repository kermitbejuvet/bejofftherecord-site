Frontwoman

Frontwoman is a multi-part biography series about women who led rock bands, and about the gap between what they made and what was written about them. Each artist gets several episodes: where she came from, the sound she made, how the press and the industry treated her, and what she left behind. So far: Debbie Harry of Blondie, Brody Dalle of The Distillers, and Shirley Manson of Garbage.

    Brody Dalle, Part 1: Punk rock has a misogyny problem

    Brody Dalle is one of the most uncompromising women ever to pick up a guitar. She wrote her own songs, fronted her own band and did not soften a single thing. Her songs ended up in Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, Spider-Man and Gran Turismo, and critics called her the real deal.

  6. Ep 6 9 May 2026

    Brody Dalle, Part 2: Melbourne, two chords and a festival stage

    Dalle grew up in working-class Fitzroy in Melbourne and has spoken publicly about a very difficult childhood. An uncle taught her two chords on an old acoustic guitar. She found Nirvana at twelve and Discharge at thirteen, formed the all-girl punk band Sourpuss at fourteen, and at sixteen played a major Australian festival.

    Shirley Manson, Part 4: Beautiful Garbage and the breaking point

    In 1999 Garbage sang The World Is Not Enough, the theme of that year's James Bond film. Their next album, Beautiful Garbage, explored gender, identity and desire in songs like Androgyny and Cherry Lips. The Androgyny video came out on 10 September 2001, the album three weeks later, and its promotion stalled in the weeks after the attacks.

    During the hiatus Manson recorded solo material that her label rejected, and in 2008 she played Catherine Weaver, a shape-shifting liquid-metal Terminator, in Terminator: The Sarah Connor Chronicles. Garbage returned in 2012 on their own label, Stunvolume, with Not Your Kind of People, followed by Strange Little Birds in 2016 and No Gods No Masters in 2021.
