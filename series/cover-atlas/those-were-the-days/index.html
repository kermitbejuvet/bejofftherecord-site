Those Were the Days: the Russian song behind the hit

"Those Were the Days" started as "Dorogoi dlinnoyu", a Russian romance from the 1920s made famous by Alexander Vertinsky. Gene Raskin wrote English lyrics, and Mary Hopkin's 1968 recording, produced by Paul McCartney, made it a worldwide hit with versions in French, Italian, German, Spanish, Turkish, Finnish and Polish.

The story

Most people hear "Those Were the Days" as an English pub song about lost youth. Its melody is Russian. "Dorogoi dlinnoyu" ("By the long road") was written in the 1920s, with music by Boris Fomin and words by Konstantin Podrevsky, and Alexander Vertinsky's recording carried it through the Russian emigre world.

In the early 1960s the American songwriter Gene Raskin wrote new English lyrics. In 1968 Paul McCartney produced it for the young Welsh singer Mary Hopkin on the new Apple label, and it went to number one in the UK. Hopkin also recorded it in other languages for different markets.

Then the covers came. Dalida made it French as "Le temps des fleurs", and versions followed in Italian, Turkish, Finnish and Polish. Almost every version keeps the same shape: a slow verse, then a chorus that speeds up like a memory you can't stop.
