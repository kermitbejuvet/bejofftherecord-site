Bang Bang (My Baby Shot Me Down): the versions around the world

Sonny Bono wrote "Bang Bang (My Baby Shot Me Down)" for Cher, who released it in 1966. The same year Nancy Sinatra recorded her darker version, Dalida sang it in Italian and Sheila in French, and the melody kept travelling from Yugoslavia and Hong Kong to Romania and the Czech Republic.

The story

"Bang Bang" is a short song about two children who play at shooting each other and grow up to be lovers, until one of them leaves. Sonny Bono wrote it and Cher released it in 1966, and it became one of her first big solo hits.

The version most people know today is not Cher's. Nancy Sinatra recorded it the same year with a bare, tremolo guitar arrangement, and in 2003 Quentin Tarantino put that recording over the opening of Kill Bill: Volume 1, which gave the song a second life.

In Europe the song moved fast. Dalida recorded it in Italian and Sheila in French in 1966, and versions followed in Yugoslavia, Hong Kong, Romania and, decades later, the Czech Republic. The melody stays almost the same in every language. What changes is the singer's distance from the story.
