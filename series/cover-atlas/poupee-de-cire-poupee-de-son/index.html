Poupée de cire, poupée de son: the Eurovision winner in every language

Serge Gainsbourg wrote "Poupée de cire, poupée de son" for France Gall, who won the 1965 Eurovision Song Contest with it for Luxembourg. Gall recorded it in German and Japanese herself, and it was covered in Czech, Danish, Finnish and Catalan.

The story

France Gall was 17 when she sang "Poupée de cire, poupée de son" at the 1965 Eurovision Song Contest. She represented Luxembourg, not France, and she won.

The lyric, by Serge Gainsbourg, is sung by a "wax doll" who sings about love without having lived it. Many listeners have read that as Gainsbourg's ironic comment on the teenage pop star singing his words, which is part of why the song is still discussed today.

The song travelled in two ways. France Gall recorded it herself in German and in Japanese, where it became one of her best known songs. Other singers covered it in Czech, Danish, Finnish and Catalan.
