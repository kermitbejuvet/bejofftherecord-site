Lambada isn't Brazilian. It's Bolivian.

The song the world knows as "Lambada" started as "Llorando se fue", recorded by the Bolivian group Los Kjarkas in 1981. It was covered in Peru and Brazil, became a worldwide hit for Kaoma in 1989, and was recorded in Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Turkish, Cantonese and Hindi.

The story

Most people remember Lambada as the sound of Brazil in 1989. The melody is older. Los Kjarkas, a folk group from Bolivia, recorded "Llorando se fue" in 1981. A Peruvian version by Cuarteto Continental followed, then a Brazilian one by Marcia Ferreira, and in 1989 the French-Brazilian group Kaoma turned it into a worldwide hit.

Kaoma's single did not credit Los Kjarkas. The original writers went to court and won. The dance on the video is Brazilian. The song is Bolivian.

Meanwhile the melody kept moving: Amr Diab sang it in Arabic in Egypt, and versions appeared in Japan, South Korea, Turkey, Hong Kong and India. In 2011 it returned once more, sampled in Jennifer Lopez and Pitbull's "On the Floor".
