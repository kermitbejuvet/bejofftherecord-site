COVER ATLAS

Cover Atlas

Cover Atlas follows one song around the world. Each episode starts with the original and then plays the versions recorded in other countries and languages, from 1960s European pop to Japanese, Turkish, Korean and Hindi adaptations. Some covers became bigger than the original. Some songs are not from where you think. Every episode below has a full page with the complete list of versions.

8 episodes

  1. Ep 1 3 October 2025

    Bang Bang (My Baby Shot Me Down): the versions around the world

    Sonny Bono wrote "Bang Bang (My Baby Shot Me Down)" for Cher, who released it in 1966. The same year Nancy Sinatra recorded her darker version, Dalida sang it in Italian and Sheila in French, and the melody kept travelling from Yugoslavia and Hong Kong to Romania and the Czech Republic.

  2. Ep 2 4 October 2025

    Strangers in the Night: the versions around the world

    "Strangers in the Night" was composed by the German bandleader Bert Kaempfert and became a number one hit for Frank Sinatra in 1966. Within a few years it had German, French, Italian, Spanish, Japanese, Turkish and Russian versions.

  3. Ep 3 5 October 2025

    Tombe la neige: the versions around the world

    "Tombe la neige" is a 1963 ballad by Salvatore Adamo, the Sicilian-born singer who grew up in Belgium. Adamo recorded it himself in Italian, German, Spanish and Japanese, and it was covered in Turkish by Ajda Pekkan and in Korean.

  4. Ep 4 8 October 2025

    Those Were the Days: the Russian song behind the hit

    "Those Were the Days" started as "Dorogoi dlinnoyu", a Russian romance from the 1920s made famous by Alexander Vertinsky. Gene Raskin wrote English lyrics, and Mary Hopkin's 1968 recording, produced by Paul McCartney, made it a worldwide hit with versions in French, Italian, German, Spanish, Turkish, Finnish and Polish.

  5. Ep 5 11 October 2025

    Poupée de cire, poupée de son: the Eurovision winner in every language

    Serge Gainsbourg wrote "Poupée de cire, poupée de son" for France Gall, who won the 1965 Eurovision Song Contest with it for Luxembourg. Gall recorded it in German and Japanese herself, and it was covered in Czech, Danish, Finnish and Catalan.

  6. Ep 6 15 October 2025

    Parole parole: the duet in every language

    "Parole parole" was first recorded in 1972 by Mina with the actor Alberto Lupo. A year later Dalida and Alain Delon made it a hit in French as "Paroles, paroles", and the duet was recorded in Portuguese, German, Spanish, Japanese and Turkish.

  7. Ep 7 19 October 2026

    Lambada isn't Brazilian. It's Bolivian.

    The song the world knows as "Lambada" started as "Llorando se fue", recorded by the Bolivian group Los Kjarkas in 1981. It was covered in Peru and Brazil, became a worldwide hit for Kaoma in 1989, and was recorded in Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Turkish, Cantonese and Hindi.

  8. Ep 8 3 November 2026

    Ya Mustafa: one Egyptian song, eight countries

    "Ya Mustafa" is an Egyptian song. Bob Azzam's French version became a European hit in 1960, and the same year it was recorded in Spanish, Danish and Japanese, followed by German, Serbo-Croatian, Catalan and, in 1994, Hindi.
