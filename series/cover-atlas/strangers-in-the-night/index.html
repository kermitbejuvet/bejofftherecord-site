Strangers in the Night: the versions around the world

"Strangers in the Night" was composed by the German bandleader Bert Kaempfert and became a number one hit for Frank Sinatra in 1966. Within a few years it had German, French, Italian, Spanish, Japanese, Turkish and Russian versions.

The story

Few people think of "Strangers in the Night" as a German song, but the melody came from Bert Kaempfert, a bandleader from Hamburg, with English lyrics by Charles Singleton and Eddie Snyder. Frank Sinatra's 1966 recording topped the charts in the United States and the United Kingdom and won him Grammy awards.

That success sent the song straight back across the Atlantic in translation. In the episode you hear it as "Fremde in der Nacht" in German, "Étrangère dans la nuit" in French and "Solo più che mai" in Italian, then in Japanese, as "İki Yabancı" in Turkey, as "Extraños en la noche" by the Mexican singer Alberto Vázquez, and finally in Russian in the Soviet Union.

The lyric is simple enough to survive any language: two strangers, one look, one night. Most versions keep Sinatra's slow swing. A few turn it into a ballad.
