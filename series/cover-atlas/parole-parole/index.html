Parole parole: the duet in every language

"Parole parole" was first recorded in 1972 by Mina with the actor Alberto Lupo. A year later Dalida and Alain Delon made it a hit in French as "Paroles, paroles", and the duet was recorded in Portuguese, German, Spanish, Japanese and Turkish.

The story

"Parole parole" is a conversation. A man speaks soft compliments over the music, and a woman answers in song that she has heard it all before: words, words, words. The Italian original, from 1972, paired the singer Mina with the actor Alberto Lupo.

The format made it easy to copy. You need one singer and one famous speaking voice. In 1973 Dalida recorded it in French with Alain Delon, and "Paroles, paroles" became one of the best known French songs of the decade.

In the episode you also hear it in Portuguese from Brazil, in German as "Worte, Worte" with Dalida again, in Spanish, in Japanese and in Turkish by Ajda Pekkan. In every version the joke is the same, and it is always on the man.
